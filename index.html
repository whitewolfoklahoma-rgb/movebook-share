How MoveBook prices a move

Volume first. The cube sheet adds up every item's cubic feet. Weight is estimated at your density setting (7 lb/cu ft is the industry planning standard for household goods). Crew size and truck come from your rules — the smallest truck that fits, and a crew matched to the volume.

Hours. Loading hours = volume ÷ (crew × your loading rate). Unloading the same way at the unloading rate. Travel time = round-trip miles ÷ average speed. Billable hours round up to the nearest half hour, the way movers actually bill.

Three prices. Break-even is your true cost: crew cost for the billable hours, truck cost for the round trip, packing materials, all in. Recommended is the higher of (a) the hourly way movers quote — billable hours × your per-mover charge, plus the flat travel fee, materials and upcharges — and (b) the price that delivers your target margin on cost. If your hourly rate would underprice an expensive job, the margin price wins and you can see why. Premium is Recommended × your premium multiplier, for weekends, rush jobs and end-of-month crunches.

Not-to-exceed. The NTE number is Recommended × your NTE add-on (default 1.10). Quote the estimate, promise the ceiling: "the estimate is $X; it will not exceed $Y." Customers love a ceiling, and the 10% cushion covers the usual surprises.

Profit floor. If you type a final price below the price that delivers your floor margin, MoveBook warns you in red and won't save until you raise the price or tick the deliberate-override box.

After the job. Enter actual hours, crew, miles, materials and what you collected on the Job Book. MoveBook computes the true profit and the hours variance — estimated vs. actual — so you can see which kinds of jobs leak hours and fix your rates.

Honest limits

MoveBook is an estimating and records tool. It does not dispatch crews, take bookings online, process payments, or replace your state's moving tariff/contract requirements — many states require specific written estimates and valuation paperwork; keep using your compliant forms for the legal documents and use MoveBook for the numbers and the records. Weight is an estimate, not a scale ticket. Data lives only in this browser on this device — export a backup before switching devices, and note that clearing browser site data erases it.

Claims & storage

Log every damage claim the day it happens — item, description, photos noted, amount claimed, how it settled. When a claim lands months later, the log is your paper trail. Storage records track who has goods with you, since when, at what monthly fee, and the total billed to date.
